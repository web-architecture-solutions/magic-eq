## (b) Standards: all current versions and dates, and which to implement

### ITU-R BS.1770: *Algorithms to measure audio programme loudness and true-peak audio level*

| Version | Date | Key change |
|---|---|---|
| BS.1770 (-0) | 2006 | K-weighting (pre-filter plus RLB high-pass), ungated Leq. Based on Soulodre 2004. |
| BS.1770-1 | 2007 | Minor revision (from memory; not confirmed). |
| BS.1770-2 | Mar 2011 | **Gating introduced**: 400 ms blocks with 75 % overlap, absolute gate at −70 LKFS, relative gate at −10 LU (confirmed). |
| BS.1770-3 | Aug 2012 | Revision (date confirmed by a secondary source). |
| BS.1770-4 | Oct 2015 | Extended channel weightings for more than 5.1 channels. This is the most widely implemented version. |
| **BS.1770-5** | **22 Nov 2023, in force** | Current version; drafted as a revision of -4. A working document dated 2024-10-22 shows a further draft revision of -5 is under way in WP6C. |

- **Implement:** BS.1770-5. For stereo and mono stems the measurement is the same as -4: K-weighting, weights G_L = G_R = 1.0, 400 ms blocks with 75 % overlap, and two-stage gating. Cite -5 as the normative reference and test against the EBU Tech 3341 test vectors.

### ISO 532: *Acoustics: Methods for calculating loudness*

| Part | Edition | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| ISO 532 (1975) | Withdrawn | Method A (Stevens) and Method B (Zwicker). Replaced by Parts 1 and 2. |
| **ISO 532-1:2017** | 1st | Zwicker method, stationary and time-varying. A corrected version was issued Nov 2017. One reseller lists it as "to be revised" (Nov 2024); ISO lists it as reviewed and confirmed in 2022. |
| **ISO 532-2:2017** | 1st | Moore-Glasberg method, **stationary only**, monaural or binaural, with informative source code. Based on Moore, Glasberg & Baer 1997 with Glasberg & Moore 2006 and Moore & Glasberg 2007. |
| **ISO 532-3:2023** | 1st, published Jul 2023 | Moore-Glasberg-Schlittenlacher method, **time-varying and binaural**, covering music and speech, with informative source code. It does not give one overall loudness value for signals longer than 5 s. Consistent with ISO 226:2003 and ISO 389-7:2019. A revision (ISO/CD 532-3) is in development. |

- **Implement:** use **ISO 532-3:2023** as the reference for any partial-loudness and binaural loudness work; it is based on Moore et al. 2016/2018. Use its source code as the test oracle.
- **Partial loudness is not normatively in ISO 532.** It comes from Moore, Glasberg & Baer 1997 (steady sounds) and Glasberg & Moore 2005 (time-varying), built on the same excitation and specific-loudness stages.

### ISO 226: *Normal equal-loudness-level contours*

| Edition | Date | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ISO/R 226 | 1961 | Recommendation, based on Robinson & Dadson 1956. |
| ISO 226 | 1987 | Found to contain large errors: up to about 15 dB below 1 kHz. |
| **ISO 226:2003** | 15 Aug 2003 (2nd ed.) | Complete revision after 18 years of work. Data from DE, DK, JP, UK and US; Japan supplied about 40 %. The underlying data and model are in Suzuki & Takeshima 2004. |
| **ISO 226:2023** | 29 Mar 2023 (current) | Aligns with ISO 389-7:2019 (threshold at 20 Hz lowered by 0.4 dB) and updates the loudness exponent and the formulas. **At most 0.6 dB different from 2003.** Annex A has graphs; Annex B has tables at third-octave frequencies from 20 Hz to 12.5 kHz. Valid for otologically normal listeners aged 18–25, binaural listening to a frontal source. |

- **Implement:** the ISO 226:2023 equations, or the 2003 tables, which are equivalent to within 0.6 dB.
- Derive a **40-phon weighting curve**, inverted and normalised to 0 dB at 1 kHz, to replace A-weighting in the masking dominance test. Interpolate between the third-octave values from 20 Hz to 12.5 kHz. Above 12.5 kHz, extrapolate or use ISO 389-7 threshold-based values.

## (c) Resolves our open items

### 1. Partial-loudness faders (Ward, Reiss & Athwal 2012)

- **Steady-state partial loudness:** `moore1997model`, MGB 1997, JAES 45:224–240.
  - Signal and masker excitation patterns are computed on the ERB-number scale.
  - Specific partial loudness N'_p is a function of excitation from the signal (E_sig) and the noise (E_noise).
  - There are separate formulas for E_sig + E_noise below and above 10^10. That threshold value is from memory; check it against the paper.
  - The AES abstract confirms the model's six stages and a "specific loudness in quiet and in noise" stage.
- **Time-varying partial loudness:** `glasberg2005development`, JAES 2005 (partial). It applies the 2002 time-varying framework (`glasberg2002model`: multi-resolution FFT, short-term and long-term loudness with attack/release smoothing) to a target in a background. **This is the formulation to implement for stems.**
- **Front-end updates:**
  - `glasberg2006prediction`: middle-ear function matching ISO 226:2003.
  - `moore2007modeling` and `moore2016loudness`: binaural inhibition.
  - `chen2011new`: an alternative excitation computation.
- **Efficiency:** `ward2013efficient` (efficient time-varying model) and `ward2015realtime` (real-time binaural meters). Same first author as the mixing paper, so likely the code lineage.
- **Validation:** **ISO 532-3:2023** source code for the loudness part. For the partial-loudness part, check against the Moore lab's published programs (not located in this search).
- **Overview:** `moore2014development`.
- **Does it pay off?** `wichern2015comparison` and the loudness-model comparisons `skovenborg2004evaluation` and `rennies2010comparison` show how much better a psychoacoustic loudness model is than BS.1770 on music.

### 2. ISO 226 contour data

- **Normative:** `iso2023normal`, ISO 226:2023. Annex B has the tables; the document includes the formula for the contour at a given phon level from the parameters α_f, L_U and T_f.
- **Background and equation derivation:** `suzuki2004equal` (JASA 2004; partial) and `suzuki2024revision` (open access, AST 45(1):1–8). The latter confirms the 2023 and 2003 editions differ by at most 0.6 dB, so 2003 tables found in open implementations are acceptable.
- **Threshold term:** `iso2019reference`, ISO 389-7:2019.
- **Model-based alternative:** `glasberg2006prediction` computes contours from the Cambridge model, so the weighting comes out consistent with partial loudness if we implement that.

### 3. A better masking model for the dominance test and the evaluation

Options, ordered from cheapest to most complete:

1. **Upgrade the current spreading function to level-dependent slopes.**
   - Terhardt 1979 slopes: lower slope about 27 dB/Bark; upper slope about −24 − 230/f + 0.2 L dB/Bark (from memory; check against PEAQ / BS.1387).
   - Add a tonality-dependent masking offset as in `johnston1988transform`.
   - References: `painter2000perceptual`, `herre2019psychoacoustic`.
2. **MPEG Psychoacoustic Model 1 or 2**, as in Ronan et al. 2018 (`iso1993mpeg1audio`, `pan1995tutorial`, `brandenburg1994isompeg1`).
   - Steps: detect tonal and non-tonal maskers, apply Bark spreading, combine into a global threshold, compute SMR per band. That gives the "MPEG-style masking model" open item directly.
   - Johnston's perceptual entropy (`johnston1988estimation`) could serve as a per-stem audibility score.
3. **PEAQ ear model** (`itu2023bs1387`, `thiede2000peaq`, `kabal2002examination`). An FFT ear model with internal noise, spreading and **temporal (forward) masking** smearing, and a noise-to-mask ratio. It is fully specified with conformance data, so it is good for reproducible evaluation.
4. **Temporal masking term:** `jesteadt1982forward` (masking linear in masker level and log delay), `zwicker1984dependence`, and the textbook treatment in `fastl2007psychoacoustics`.
5. **Partial loudness as the masking measure:** the MGB 1997 / G&M 2005 partial-to-unmasked loudness ratio is a more principled "how masked is this stem" score. It also connects to `aichinger2011describing`, the masked-to-unmasked ratio.
6. **Gold-standard offline judge** (not real-time):
   - `dau1996quantitative` and `dau1997modeling` (PEMO with modulation filterbank), `jepsen2008computational` (CASP), `biberger2016envelope` (GPSM, lighter).
   - Reference code is in the AMT (`majdak2022amt`).
   - Full-reference evaluation against an engineer's mix: `huber2006pemoq` (PEMO-Q) and `hines2015visqolaudio` / `chinen2020visqol` (open source).
   - Metric choice: `torcoli2021objective`.

Comparators from the mixing literature: `vega2010quantifying`, `aichinger2011describing`, `hafezi2015autonomous` (our current measure), `wakefield2015investigation` (listening-test ground truth), `ford2015mixviz` (visualisation), `jillings2017automatic` and `ronan2018automatic` (optimisers).

### 4. A binaural / stereo-aware measure

- **Spatial release from masking:**
  - `bronkhorst2000cocktail`, `bronkhorst2015cocktail` and `culling2021binaural` give the size of the effect: several dB, up to about 10+ dB for speech.
  - `culling2021binaural` separates **better-ear** listening from **binaural unmasking**.
- **Computable model:**
  - `lavandier2010prediction`: per-band better-ear SNR plus a binaural-unmasking advantage from the interaural coherence and phase of the masker (the equalization-cancellation idea, `durlach1963equalization`).
  - For stems panned with level differences only, a **better-ear SMR (computed separately for the L and R mix buses, taking the maximum per band)** is a cheap first step.
  - The EC/BMLD term (`hirsh1948influence`, `durlach1963equalization`) matters mainly for decorrelated or phase-differing sources such as stereo reverbs and wide synths.
- **Binaural loudness:** `moore2007modeling`, `moore2016loudness`, `moore2018testing` and ISO 532-3 (binaural inhibition) sum per-ear loudness correctly for panned stems.
- **Mixing prior art:** `tom2019automatic` (unmasking-driven stereo panning). The MPEG stereo models in `herre2019psychoacoustic` (BMLD in M/S coding) are also relevant.
- **Caveats for the evaluation:** informational masking (`kidd2008informational`, `brungart2001informational`, `durlach2003note`) and CMR (`hall1984detection`, `verhey2003psychophysics`) are effects energy-based measures cannot capture. Use them to bound the claims in the write-up.

## (d) Notable facts with sources

- **Bark formula.** The Zwicker & Terhardt 1980 closed form matches the critical-band rate within 0.2 Bark and the critical bandwidth within 10 % over the whole audible range. It has no closed-form inverse, so implementations often use Traunmüller 1990 for that (Völk, DAGA 2015, via search). [`zwicker1980analytical`, `traunmuller1990analytical`]
- **Number of critical bands.** The 24 classic critical bands reach only about 15.5 kHz, so more bands are needed to cover 20 kHz (AUDITORY list discussion, 2023). [`zwicker1961subdivision`]
- **Notched noise.** Patterson's notched-noise filters had 3-dB bandwidths of about 0.13 × the centre frequency, with a roughly Gaussian passband. [`patterson1976auditory`]
- **ERB.** Glasberg & Moore 1990 gives the ERB equations versus centre frequency and level; it is also cited for an outer/middle-ear correction based on minimum audible pressure (MAP). [`glasberg1990derivation`]
- **Moore & Glasberg 1983.** Its ERB polynomial is valid from 0.1 to 6.5 kHz at moderate levels; the 1990 paper replaced it with a linear form. [`moore1983suggested`]
- **Fletcher 1940.** The band-widening experiment is fairly insensitive and underestimated bandwidths, but the assumptions about filter shape and spacing held up (Patterson's 1995 review, via search). [`fletcher1940auditory`]
- **Gammatone.** Its amplitude response closely matches the roex shape that predicts masking data, and it has an efficient recursive implementation. [`patterson1987efficient`]
- **Schroeder, Atal & Hall 1979.** The core idea is to make coding noise inaudible by hiding it under the masking threshold rather than minimising its power; this is the design principle behind MP3 and AAC. [`schroeder1979optimizing`]
- **Johnston 1988.** A 4 bit/sample transform coder using a tonality-dependent masking threshold was transparent at 128 kb/s (mono, 32 kHz), and probably at 96 kb/s. [`johnston1988transform`]
- **Forward masking.** Masking in dB grows with masker level and with log signal delay. Low frequencies show slightly more forward masking at equal sensation level. [`jesteadt1982forward`]
- **MGB 1997.** Described as the most-cited JAES paper (REF impact case study). It is used industrially for HVAC, vehicle and aircraft noise. Moore quotes **8.1 sones for a 70 dB, 1 kHz tone** (AUDITORY list, 2000), and implementations can differ (old software or third-octave input). [`moore1997model`]
- **Binaural loudness.** The level difference for equal loudness between diotic and monaural presentation is about 5–6 dB, not 10 dB, so a diotic sound is about 1.5 times as loud as the same sound in one ear. Glasberg & Moore 2002 assumed full summation across ears; binaural inhibition fixes this. [`moore2007modeling`, `moore2016loudness`]
- **Glasberg & Moore 2002 limitation.** Its short-term loudness did not distinguish a signal containing shocks from a phase-randomised signal with the same long-term spectrum (2011 JASA letter, via search). [`glasberg2002model`]
- **ISO 532-3:2023.** It covers tones, noise, music, speech and impact sounds; gives no single overall loudness value for signals longer than 5 s; and is consistent with ISO 226:2003 and ISO 389-7:2019. [`iso2023loudness3`]
- **ISO 226 revisions.** The 1987 contours were off by up to about 15 dB below 1 kHz (AIST 2003). The 2023 edition differs from 2003 by at most 0.6 dB. [`iso2003normal`, `suzuki2024revision`]
- **BS.1770 origin.** Soulodre 2004 formally evaluated 10 submitted loudness meters against listening tests at 5 sites. Skovenborg & Nielsen 2004 evaluated 12 loudness models on music and speech. These evaluations led to the simple K-weighted Leq. [`soulodre2004evaluation`, `skovenborg2004evaluation`]
- **EBU Mode timescales.** Momentary 400 ms, short-term 3 s, integrated over the whole programme. The integrated-loudness relative gate is −10 LU. The Tech 3342 changelog says v2 (Aug 2011) changed "the relative gate from −8 to −10 LU", matching BS.1770-2. The LRA's own −20 LU relative gate on short-term values is from memory; check it in the Tech 3342 PDF. [`ebu2023tech3341`, `ebu2023tech3342`]
- **PEAQ.** It maps model output variables to an Objective Difference Grade (ODG) with a 3-layer perceptron, in basic and advanced versions. It has documented limits on bandwidth extension and parametric codecs. [`thiede2000peaq`, `delgado2020can`]
- **PEMO-Q.** Built on the Dau et al. model; it uses cross-correlation of internal representations, giving the perceptual similarity measure (PSM). [`huber2006pemoq`]
- **Dau models.**
  - The 1997 modulation filterbank has constant 5 Hz bandwidth from 0–10 Hz and constant Q = 2 from 10 to 1000 Hz.
  - The 1996 model predicts masking data within a few dB, except for forward masking by brief maskers and threshold changes with signal duration.
  - [`dau1997modeling`, `dau1996quantitative2`]
- **CASP.** Jepsen et al. 2008 adds DRNL cochlear compression to the Dau model and was validated on intensity discrimination, tone-in-noise detection, spectral and forward masking, and AM detection. [`jepsen2008computational`]
- **CMR.** A tone masked by modulated noise becomes more detectable when a remote noise band carries the same modulation. Relevant for rhythm-section stems that share envelopes. [`hall1984detection`]
- **BMLD / EC theory.** Durlach 1963 equalises and subtracts the two ears' signals; the 1972 revision equalises interaural delay only. The theory fits a wide range of binaural detection data. [`durlach1963equalization`]
- **Timbre dimensions.** McAdams et al. 1995 found three shared dimensions (log rise time, spectral centroid, spectral flux), plus sound-specific "specificities" and five latent listener classes. [`mcadams1995perceptual`]
- **Bregman 1990.** Defines the "auditory stream", sequential versus simultaneous integration, and primitive (innate) versus schema-based grouping. [`bregman1990auditory`]
- **ViSQOLAudio.** Compared with PEAQ and POLQA on low-bitrate music. ViSQOL v3 is open-source C++ (github.com/google/visqol). [`hines2015visqolaudio`, `chinen2020visqol`]
